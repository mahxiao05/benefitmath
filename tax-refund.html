Common questions

Wondering when it arrives? Read When will I get my tax refund?.

Want more on one credit? See the child tax credit and EITC calculators, which add Oregon and Washington credits too.

How the refund is figured

Your refund is the federal tax taken out of your paychecks, plus any refundable credits, minus the income tax you owe after credits. This page adds up your income, subtracts the standard deduction and the new senior, tips and overtime deductions, figures the tax with the 2026 brackets, then applies the child tax credit and the Earned Income Tax Credit.

When will I get my 2026 refund?

The IRS usually starts taking 2026 returns in late January 2027. Most e-filed returns with direct deposit are paid within 21 days. By law, refunds that include the EITC or the additional child tax credit are held until mid-February, so those usually arrive in early March.

Where do I find how much tax was withheld?

On your W-2, box 2 shows the federal income tax withheld for the year. Before you get your W-2, your last pay stub of the year shows it as year-to-date federal withholding. Add any estimated tax payments you made.

Why does it say I owe?

If less tax was withheld than you owe, the difference is due with your return. Common reasons are a second job, self-employment income, unemployment benefits without withholding, or a W-4 that claims too many credits.

Does a tax refund change my SNAP or SSI?

No. Federal tax refunds don't count as income for SNAP, SSI, Medicaid or housing aid, and they aren't counted as savings for 12 months after you get them.

What does this leave out?

Self-employment tax, itemized deductions, education and child care credits, the premium tax credit, capital gains rates, and state income tax. If any of those apply to you, use this as a starting point and check your return.

Tips and overtime

New to this? Read No tax on tips and overtime, explained.

How the tips deduction works

From 2025 through 2028, workers in jobs that customarily get tips can deduct up to $25,000 of tips a year from their income before federal income tax. It works whether you take the standard deduction or itemize.

  1. Add up the tips you got from customers, including tips shared with you through a tip pool.
  2. Cap it at $25,000. If you're self-employed, it also can't be more than your profit from that work.
  3. The cap shrinks by $100 for every $1,000 of income over $150,000 ($300,000 joint).
  4. Your tax drops by the deduction times your bracket rate.
Which jobs count?

The IRS published a list of occupations that customarily received tips as of December 31, 2024, such as servers, bartenders, hairstylists and delivery drivers. If your job isn't on the list, your tips don't qualify.

Do I still pay Social Security tax on tips?

Yes. The deduction only lowers federal income tax. Social Security and Medicare tax still apply. Oregon lets you subtract the same tip deduction on your Oregon return (Schedule OR-ASC), and Washington has no income tax.

Do service charges count?

No. Mandatory service charges and automatic gratuities set by the business are wages, not tips.

Does this affect SNAP or SSI?

No. Those programs count your tips as earned income either way.

How the overtime deduction works

From 2025 through 2028 you can deduct the extra pay you get for overtime that federal law requires. If you earn $20 an hour and get $30 for an overtime hour, only the extra $10 is deducted. The $20 is still taxed like regular pay.

  1. Find your overtime pay for the year. Your W-2 or final pay stub may show a separate qualified overtime amount.
  2. Divide time-and-a-half pay by 3 to get the extra half.
  3. Cap it at $12,500, or $25,000 on a joint return. The cap shrinks by $100 for every $1,000 of income over $150,000 ($300,000 joint).
  4. Subtract it from your income. Your tax drops by that amount times your bracket rate, usually 10% or 12% for most hourly workers.
Is all my overtime tax-free now?

No. Only the extra half of time-and-a-half counts, and it's a deduction, not an exemption. Social Security and Medicare still apply. Oregon lets you subtract the same amount on your Oregon return (Schedule OR-ASC); Washington has no income tax. Many workers save a few hundred dollars a year.

Does overtime from my state or union contract count?

Only overtime required by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act counts, which means hours over 40 in a workweek. Daily overtime required only by state law, or extra premiums in a union contract, don't qualify.

I'm salaried. Do I qualify?

Only if you are nonexempt, meaning your employer must pay you overtime under federal law. Exempt salaried workers don't get FLSA overtime, so they have nothing to deduct.

Do I have to itemize?

No. You can take it with the standard deduction.

Will more overtime cut my SNAP or SSI?

It can. Benefits count your gross pay, and this tax deduction doesn't change that. Check the boxes in the calculator to see the effect.

The $6,000 senior deduction

New to this? Read The $6,000 senior deduction, explained.

How the senior deduction works

From 2025 through 2028, each person who is 65 or older by the end of the year can deduct up to $6,000 more, on top of the regular standard deduction and the existing extra amount for age 65. A married couple who are both 65+ can deduct up to $12,000.

It shrinks by 6% of income over $75,000 ($150,000 joint). It doesn't remove tax on Social Security. It lowers taxable income, so it only helps if you would otherwise owe federal income tax.

Is Social Security tax-free now?

No. The law added this deduction instead. Many retirees with modest income already paid no tax on Social Security, and for them this deduction may not change anything.

Do I have to be retired or getting Social Security?

No. Only your age matters: 65 or older by December 31 of the tax year.

Can I take it if I itemize?

Yes. It is available with the standard deduction or itemized deductions.

What if only one spouse is 65?

On a joint return, only the spouse who is 65+ gets the $6,000. Pick "One" in the calculator.

Withholding and your W-4

What does this tell me?

Whether the federal tax taken out of your pay will cover what you owe for 2026. If it takes out too much, you get a refund. Too little, and you owe when you file.

How do I change my withholding?

Give your employer a new Form W-4. To take out more, write an extra amount per paycheck in Step 4(c). To take out less, claim your children in Step 3. You can change it any time.

Is a big refund bad?

Not bad, but it means you lent the government money all year for free. Some people like the forced savings. Others would rather have the money in each paycheck.

Do I owe a penalty if I owe at tax time?

Usually not if you owe less than $1,000, or if withholding covered at least 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's.

Tax brackets

What is a tax bracket?

The U.S. taxes income in layers. The first slice of taxable income is taxed at 10%, the next slice at 12%, and so on. Your bracket is the rate on your last dollar, not on all of your income.

Will a raise push me into a higher bracket and lower my pay?

No. Only the dollars above the bracket line are taxed at the higher rate. A raise always leaves you with more money after federal income tax.

What is the standard deduction for 2026?

$16,100 single, $32,200 married filing jointly and $24,150 head of household. People 65 or older get an extra $2,050 (single) or $1,650 each (married).

What is my effective tax rate?

Your total federal income tax divided by your total income. It is almost always much lower than your bracket.

What are the 2026 capital gains rates?

Long-term gains are taxed at 0% for taxable income up to $49,450 single, $98,900 married jointly or $66,200 head of household; 15% up to $545,500, $613,700 or $579,600; and 20% above that. This calculator treats all income as regular income.
