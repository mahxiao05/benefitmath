The Oregon Kicker, Explained

When Oregon collects more tax than expected, it gives the extra back. That's the “kicker.” Here's how it works and whether you can still get it.

1What the kicker is

Oregon's law says that if income tax collections come in more than 2% above the forecast, the extra goes back to taxpayers. It's figured every two years.

Your share is a percentage of the Oregon tax you paid. For the last kicker, it was 9.863% of your 2024 tax. If your 2024 Oregon tax was $3,000, your kicker was about $296.

2No kicker on your 2026 return

The return you file in early 2027 (for tax year 2026) has no kicker. That's normal. Kickers only come on odd-year returns.

If your Oregon refund looks smaller than last year, this may be why.

3Didn't get the last one? You still can

  • You qualify if you had Oregon tax on a 2024 return, even if you've moved away.
  • To claim it, file a 2025 Oregon return, even if you wouldn't otherwise need to.
  • If you didn't file for 2024, file that first. Full-year residents can use Direct File Oregon for free.

Oregon can use the kicker to pay debts you owe the state, like back taxes or child support. You can also give it to Oregon schools by checking a box.

4Will there be a kicker in 2028?

Not right now. Oregon's August 2026 forecast doesn't expect one for the 2025–27 budget period. Forecasts change every quarter, so this could change.

To look up your exact past kicker, use “What's My Kicker?” on Oregon's Revenue Online site.

5Common questions

Is the kicker taxed?

Oregon doesn't tax it. It counts on your federal return only if you itemized and deducted state taxes before.

Do Washington residents get a kicker?

Only if they paid Oregon income tax, for example by working in Oregon.

Will I get a separate check?

No. It adds to your refund or lowers what you owe on your Oregon return.
