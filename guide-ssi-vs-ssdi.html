SSI vs. SSDI: What's the Difference?

Same agency, same definition of disability. Almost everything else is different.

At a glance

$994Most SSI pays a month in 2026
$2,000SSI savings limit for one person
5 monthsSSDI waiting period
  • SSDI is insurance you earned by working. The amount depends on your past pay.
  • SSI is for people with very little income and savings. It pays a set amount, minus other income.
  • You can get both if your SSDI is small.

2Can you get both?

Yes. If your SSDI is under $994, SSI can fill part of the gap. SSI counts SSDI as income, so after the first $20, each dollar of SSDI lowers SSI by a dollar.

3What happens when you work

  • SSI ignores the first $20 of income, the first $65 of earnings and half the rest. Working always leaves you with more money.
  • SSDI doesn't shrink as you earn. Months over $1,210 use one of 9 trial work months. After those, no check in months over $1,690 ($2,830 if blind).

Health coverage usually outlasts the cash. Medicaid can continue under SSI's 1619(b) rule up to $47,856 a year in Oregon or $73,897 in Washington (2026). Medicare continues at least 93 months after SSDI's trial work period.

4How to apply

Apply through Social Security at ssa.gov/disability, by phone at 1-800-772-1213, or at a local office. Decisions can take months, so apply early. If you're turned down, you can appeal.

For SNAP and health coverage, use ONE Oregon or Washington Connection.
