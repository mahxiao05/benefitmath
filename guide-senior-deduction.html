The $6,000 Senior Deduction, Explained

If you're 65 or older, a new deduction can lower your federal taxes for 2025 through 2028. Here's what it does and doesn't do.

At a glance

$6,000Extra deduction per person 65+
$12,000For a married couple who are both 65+
$75,000Income where it starts to shrink (single)
  • Only your age matters. You don't have to be retired or on Social Security.
  • It only helps if you'd owe federal income tax without it.
  • It does not make Social Security tax-free.
Who gets itWhat it savesIncome limitsMythsQuestions

1Who gets it

  • You're 65 or older by December 31 of the tax year.
  • You have a Social Security number valid for work.
  • If you're married, you file jointly. Married people filing separately can't take it.

It comes on top of the regular standard deduction and the extra amount you already get for being 65. You can take it whether you itemize or not.

2What it saves you

A deduction lowers your taxable income, not your tax directly. How much you save depends on your tax bracket.

Your tax bracketTax saved by a $6,000 deduction
10%$600
12%$720
22%$1,320

If your income is already below your standard deduction, you owe no federal tax, and this deduction can't save you anything more.

3Income limits

The deduction shrinks by 6% of income over $75,000 for single filers, or $150,000 for married couples filing jointly.

Example: a single person with $95,000 of income is $20,000 over. 6% of that is $1,200, so the deduction drops to $4,800. It's gone completely at $175,000 for single filers.

4What it isn't

  • It isn't “no tax on Social Security.” Social Security can still be taxed. The law added this deduction instead.
  • It isn't permanent. It's for tax years 2025 through 2028 unless Congress extends it.
  • It doesn't change Oregon taxes. It's a federal deduction. Oregon already doesn't tax Social Security.

5Common questions

What if only one spouse is 65?

On a joint return, only the spouse who is 65 or older gets the $6,000.

Do I need to do anything special to claim it?

Tax software adds it when you enter your birth date. On paper, it goes on the new Schedule 1-A.

Can I also take the tips or overtime deductions?

Yes, if you qualify. They stack on top of each other.
