Paid Leave Oregon vs. Washington PFML

Both pay part of your wages while you heal, care for family or bond with a new baby. The details differ.

At a glance

$1,692Oregon weekly maximum (benefit years from June 28, 2026)
$1,647Washington weekly maximum (2026)
12 weeksUsual leave in either state, more for pregnancy
  • The program follows where you work, not where you live.
  • Oregon usually pays more on lower wages; Washington has an unpaid first week for most leave.
  • Neither state withholds taxes from benefits automatically.

2Which state pays more?

On lower wages, usually Oregon: it replaces 100% of pay up to 65% of the state average, versus Washington's 90% up to half. At high wages the maximums are close.

3Is it taxed? Does it affect benefits?

Neither state withholds taxes automatically. Ask the agency about federal withholding, and check IRS guidance or a tax preparer. Paid leave counts as income for SSI and SNAP.

4How to apply

Oregon: Paid Leave Oregon. Washington: Washington Paid Family and Medical Leave. Both premiums count wages up to the Social Security wage base, $184,500 in 2026.
