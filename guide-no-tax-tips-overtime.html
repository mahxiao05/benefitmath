No Tax on Tips and Overtime: Who Qualifies

Two federal deductions cut income tax on tips and overtime from 2025 through 2028. They don't make that pay tax-free, but they can save you hundreds.

At a glance

$25,000Most tips you can deduct a year
$12,500Most overtime you can deduct ($25,000 joint)
2025–2028Tax years covered
  • They lower federal income tax only. Social Security and Medicare still apply.
  • You get them with the standard deduction. No itemizing needed.
  • Oregon lets you subtract the same amounts on your Oregon return.
TipsOvertimeRulesHow to claimOR & WA

1Tips: who qualifies

  • Your job is on the IRS list of occupations that customarily got tips as of December 31, 2024, like servers, bartenders, hairstylists and delivery drivers.
  • Voluntary tips from customers count, including tips shared through a tip pool. Mandatory service charges and automatic gratuities don't.
  • If you're self-employed, the deduction can't be more than your profit from that work.

2Overtime: what counts

  • Only the extra half of time-and-a-half that federal law requires. Earn $20 an hour and get $30 for overtime? Only $10 is deducted.
  • Federal overtime means hours over 40 in a workweek. Daily overtime under state law and extra union-contract premiums don't count.
  • You must be nonexempt, meaning your employer has to pay you overtime under federal law.

3Rules for both

  • You need a valid Social Security number.
  • If you're married, you must file jointly.
  • Above $150,000 of modified AGI ($300,000 joint), the cap shrinks by $100 for each $1,000 over.

4How to claim it

Claim it on your federal return using the new Schedule 1-A. For 2025, employers didn't have to list qualified overtime separately on your W-2, so keep your pay stubs and any statement your employer gives you about tips or overtime.

5Oregon and Washington

Oregon: claim the same tip and overtime deductions as subtractions on Schedule OR-ASC with your Oregon return. Nonresidents can only count tips and overtime earned while working in Oregon.

Washington: no income tax, so there's nothing to claim. If you get SNAP or SSI, these programs still count your full pay.
