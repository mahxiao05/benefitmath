The EITC in Oregon and Washington

Working families can stack a state credit on top of the federal Earned Income Tax Credit. Here's what each pays and how to claim them.

At a glance

$8,231Top federal EITC for 2026 (3+ children)
9% or 12%Oregon's credit, as a share of your federal EITC
$50–$1,330Washington's credit for tax year 2025
  • The federal EITC is refundable: you can get it back even if you owe no tax.
  • Oregon adds a percentage of your federal credit on your Oregon return.
  • Washington pays its own credit through a separate application.

2Oregon's earned income credit

  • 12% of your federal EITC if you have a child under 3, otherwise 9%. It's claimed on your Oregon return.
  • Filers with an ITIN can claim it too, using Schedule OR-EIC-ITIN.
  • Families with young kids may also get the Oregon Kids Credit: $1,050 per child age 0 to 5 for 2025, for lower incomes.

Example: a federal EITC of $7,316 adds $658 in Oregon, or $878 with a child under 3.

3Washington's Working Families Tax Credit

  • For tax year 2025: up to $335 with no children, $660 with one, $995 with two and $1,330 with three or more. Anyone who qualifies gets at least $50.
  • You need an SSN or ITIN, to have lived in Washington at least 183 days, a federal return on file, and to qualify for the federal EITC (or would, except for using an ITIN).
  • Apply separately at workingfamiliescredit.wa.gov. Each year can be claimed for about four years.
