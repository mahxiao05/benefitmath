Common questions

Not sure which benefit you have? Read SSI vs. SSDI: what's the difference?

How much can I earn on SSI?

SSI doesn't count the first $20 of income, the first $65 of earnings, and half of the rest. With the 2026 maximum of $994, each $2 you earn above about $85 a month lowers SSI by about $1, so working always leaves you with more. For someone with no other income, SSI stops at about $2,073 a month in earnings, and Medicaid can continue well past that.

How much can I earn on SSDI?

SSDI doesn't shrink as you earn. Instead, it watches two numbers. In 2026, any month you earn over $1,210 uses one of 9 trial work months, and SSDI keeps paying in full. After those 9 months, SSDI isn't paid for months when your earnings, minus disability-related work costs, are over $1,690 ($2,830 if blind).

What happens after my trial work period?

A 36-month extended period of eligibility starts. SSDI is paid for any month you earn under the limit and stops for months over it. The first month over the limit, plus the next two, are still paid as a grace period. If your benefits end and you have to stop working within 5 years, you can ask for expedited reinstatement instead of reapplying.

Will I lose Medicaid or Medicare if I work?

Usually not right away. If you got SSI, Section 1619(b) keeps Medicaid while your earnings stay under a state threshold: $47,856 a year in Oregon and $73,897 in Washington for 2026. With SSDI, Medicare continues for at least 93 months after your trial work period ends, as long as you still have a disability.

What are impairment-related work expenses?

Costs you pay yourself because of your disability so you can work, such as a job coach, special transportation, or medication. SSA subtracts them from your earnings when deciding the SSDI limit, and excludes them for SSI. Keep receipts and tell SSA.

Can I save money without losing SSI?

Yes, with an ABLE account. The first $100,000 in one doesn't count toward SSI's $2,000 resource limit. Starting in 2026, your disability must have begun before age 46, and up to $20,000 a year can go in.

Do I have to report my earnings?

Yes. Report your pay to Social Security every month for SSI, and report work promptly for SSDI. Late reporting is the most common cause of overpayments you'd have to pay back.

What if I get both SSI and SSDI?

SSDI counts as unearned income for SSI, so after the $20 exclusion it lowers SSI dollar for dollar. Your earnings then follow both sets of rules at once. Choose "Both" above to see the combined result.
