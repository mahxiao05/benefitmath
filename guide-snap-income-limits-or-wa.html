SNAP Income Limits in Oregon and Washington (2026)

Who qualifies for food stamps in Oregon and Washington's Basic Food program, and how much you can get.

At a glance

$2,660Income limit for 1 person, per month
$5,500Income limit for a family of 4
$1,023Most SNAP pays a family of 4
  • Both states allow income up to about 200% of the poverty level, before taxes.
  • There's no asset test for most households under the limit.
  • Over the limit? Households with someone 60+ or disabled can still qualify.

2How your benefit is figured

  • Start with income before taxes, then take off 20% of pay from work, a standard deduction ($217 for 1 to 3 people) and child care costs.
  • Housing: rent plus your state's utility allowance ($533 if you pay for heat or cooling). The part above half your remaining income is deducted, up to $769.
  • Your benefit is the maximum for your household size minus 30% of what's left. Households of 1 or 2 that qualify get at least $25.

That's why two families with the same income can get very different amounts: rent and utilities matter a lot.

3If someone is 60+ or has a disability

  • No cap on the housing deduction.
  • Medical costs over $35 a month are deducted.
  • Over the regular limit, you can still qualify with net income under 100% of poverty ($1,330 for 1 person) and assets under $4,750.

4Work rules

Most adults 18 to 64 must register for work. Adults without children under 14 at home can be limited to 3 months of SNAP in 3 years unless they work or train 20 hours a week or are exempt, for example because of a disability, pregnancy, or caring for someone who can't care for themselves.

Read the full work rules guide.

5How to apply

Oregon: ONE Oregon online, 800-699-9075, or a local office.

Washington: Washington Connection online, 877-501-2233, or a Community Services Office.

Both states need a short interview, usually by phone. Benefits arrive each month on an EBT card.
