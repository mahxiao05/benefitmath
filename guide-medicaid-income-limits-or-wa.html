OHP and Apple Health Income Limits

If your income is low enough, you can get health coverage at no cost in Oregon or Washington. Here are the limits, in monthly dollars.

At a glance

$1,835About the monthly limit for one adult in 2026
$0Monthly premium on OHP and Apple Health for most adults
Any timeYou can apply all year, not just in open enrollment
  • Adults under 65 qualify with income up to about 138% of the poverty level.
  • Limits are higher for children and pregnant people.
  • If you earn a bit too much, you can still get help paying for a marketplace plan.

This is income before taxes come out. It uses 138% of the 2026 federal poverty level. The states round a little differently, so treat these as close estimates.

In Oregon this program is OHP (Oregon Health Plan). In Washington it's Apple Health. Both are Medicaid.

2Higher limits for children and pregnancy

WhoOregonWashington
Children under 19Up to 305% of povertyUp to 317% (small premium above 215%)
Pregnant peopleUp to 190% of povertyUp to 215% of poverty

For a family of four, 305% of poverty is roughly $8,400 a month. So many working families who earn too much for adult coverage can still get free or low-cost coverage for their kids.

3If you earn a little too much

  • Oregon: adults earning up to 200% of poverty can get OHP Bridge, which also has no monthly cost.
  • Both states: the marketplace (OregonHealthCare.gov or Washington Healthplanfinder) offers tax credits that lower plan prices for people up to 400% of poverty.

Most people 65 and older get Medicare instead, and there's separate help for Medicare costs.

4How to apply

  • Oregon: apply online at benefits.oregon.gov, by phone, or with a free local helper (a community partner).
  • Washington: apply at wahealthplanfinder.org or through the Washington Healthplanfinder app.

You can apply any time of year. Have pay stubs and Social Security numbers handy. One application can also check you for SNAP in Oregon.

5Common questions

Do they count my savings?

Not for most adults, children or pregnant people. Savings limits mostly apply to people 65 and older or with disabilities.

Does Oregon or Washington use my monthly or yearly income?

Medicaid looks at your current monthly income. Marketplace tax credits look at your expected income for the whole year.

What if my income goes up later?

Report the change. If you no longer qualify, you can move to a marketplace plan without waiting for open enrollment.
