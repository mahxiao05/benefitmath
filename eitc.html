How the EITC works

The Earned Income Tax Credit is for people who work and earn low to moderate pay. It's refundable, so it can come back to you as a refund even if you owe no income tax.

  1. Phase-in: the credit grows with each dollar you earn, up to a maximum that depends on how many children you have.
  2. Plateau: you get the maximum while income stays below the phase-out start.
  3. Phase-out: the credit shrinks as the greater of your AGI or earned income rises, until it reaches zero.

For 2026 the maximum is $664 with no children, $4,427 with one, $7,316 with two and $8,231 with three or more.

Can I get the EITC without children?

Yes, if you are 25 to 64 and your income is low enough. The amount is smaller, up to $664 for 2026.

Do disability benefits count as earned income?

SSI and SSDI don't count as earned income. Disability pension from an employer plan can count until you reach your plan's minimum retirement age.

Will my EITC refund cut my SNAP or SSI?

No. Federal tax refunds aren't counted as income, and they aren't counted as savings for 12 months.

Where can I file for free?

IRS VITA sites prepare returns for free for people with moderate income, and many help with the EITC. Find a site near you or call 800-906-9887.
