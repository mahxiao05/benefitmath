What Can You Buy With SNAP?

The short version: food to take home and eat, yes. Hot food, alcohol and things that aren't food, no.

At a glance

YesGroceries, snacks, drinks, seeds and food plants
NoHot food, alcohol, vitamins, pet food
No limitsNo soda or candy ban in Oregon or Washington
  • If it has a Nutrition Facts label, it's usually OK. A Supplement Facts label means no.
  • Hot at the register means no, even at a grocery store.
  • Farmers markets can double your SNAP for fruits and vegetables.
Can buyCan't buyStretch itRestaurants

1What you can buy

  • Fruits and vegetables, fresh, frozen or canned
  • Meat, poultry and fish, including live shellfish
  • Milk, cheese, eggs, bread, cereal, rice and pasta
  • Snacks, sweets and non-alcoholic drinks
  • Seeds and plants that grow food, like tomato starts or a basil plant

Neither Oregon nor Washington has a federal waiver limiting soda or candy. About two dozen other states do.

2What you can't buy

  • Beer, wine, liquor, cigarettes and tobacco, or anything with cannabis or CBD
  • Vitamins, medicine and supplements (anything with a Supplement Facts label)
  • Food that's hot when you pay for it, like a hot rotisserie chicken or deli meals
  • Live animals, other than shellfish and fish
  • Things that aren't food: pet food, paper towels, soap, diapers, toothpaste, cosmetics

3Get more for your SNAP

  • Oregon: Double Up Food Bucks matches SNAP for fruits and vegetables at many farmers markets and some stores.
  • Washington: SNAP Market Match gives you extra money for fruits and vegetables at participating markets.
  • Online: many big grocery stores take EBT online. You can't use SNAP for delivery fees.

4Can I use SNAP at restaurants?

Not yet in Oregon or Washington. Oregon is building a Restaurant Meals Program for people 60+, people with a disability and people without housing. It will start in a few test areas. We'll add it here when it opens.
