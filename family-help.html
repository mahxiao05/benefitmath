Common questions

How Washington figures a TANF grant
  1. Start with the payment standard for your household size.
  2. From your earnings, subtract $500, then half of what's left. The rest counts as income.
  3. The grant is the payment standard minus your counted income.
Where do I apply?

TANF: Oregon through ODHS at ONE Oregon, Washington through DSHS at Washington Connection. WIC: through a local clinic. Find one in Oregon or Washington. Energy help: through your local agency. Find yours in Oregon or Washington.

Does SNAP count here?

SNAP has its own rules. Use the SNAP calculator to see how much you could get.

Child care help

Where do I apply?

Oregon: through the Department of Early Learning and Care. Apply online through ONE Oregon, call 800-699-9075, or see the ERDC page for other ways to apply. ERDC has a waitlist, so it's worth getting on it early. Washington: apply for Working Connections Child Care online through Washington Connection or by calling DCYF at 844-626-8687.

Why can I keep help at a higher income than I need to start?

Both states let families who already get help keep it as their income grows, up to a higher limit, so a raise doesn't mean suddenly losing child care.

Does the copay depend on how many kids are in care?

In Washington, the copay on DCYF's chart is set by family size and income. Check with your caseworker for your exact amount.
